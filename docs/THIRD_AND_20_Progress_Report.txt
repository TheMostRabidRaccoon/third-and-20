Section 4: The Vision - Neutral Fact-Finder
The ultimate goal is to build a physics-first, neutral fact-finding system that differs fundamentally from existing football analytics. This section outlines what that means and what must be built to achieve it.
Core Architecture Requirements
The neutral fact-finder requires three foundational capabilities:
1. Canonical Field Coordinates
Transform all video into a standardized field coordinate system (0-120 yards x 0-53.3 yards). This eliminates camera-relative left/right noise and enables comparison across plays regardless of which direction teams are facing.
Detect field markings (hashes, yard lines, numbers)
Fit homography to map pixel (u,v) to field (x,y) in yards
Continuously update if camera pans/zooms
2. Possession + Game State as Derived Facts
Rather than assuming team perspective, infer game state from the data:
possession_team_id (from scorebug OCR or formation inference)
los_x (line of scrimmage in field coordinates)
first_down_x (line-to-gain)
drive_dir (computed from ball movement post-snap)
3. Two-Phase Architecture
To meet the 12-second play-call window, separate offline and online computation:
Offline (Pregame/Batch):
Field calibration model per stadium/camera
Roster/jersey mapping priors
Team tendencies, formation clustering, coverage families
Opponent scouting models from public film
Online (In-Game 12s Loop):
Play segmentation (snap detection)
Formation ID + motion detection
Lightweight coverage/pressure indicators
Situational match to precomputed decision table

Section 6: Why Neutral Fact-Finding Matters
The fundamental difference between THIRD & 20 and existing products:
Legacy Approach (Hudl, PFF, etc.)
Film review first, automation bolted on later
Team-centric data model ("my team vs opponent")
Direction, offense/defense assumed, not derived
Manual tagging disguised as analytics
Cannot support real-time decisions
THIRD & 20 Approach (Physics-First)
Physical reality first: bodies, trajectories, constraints, time
State inference: possession, formation, motion, pressure derived
Game theory layer: tendencies, counters, exploitation windows
"Team perspective" is a rendering choice, not a data model
Enables 12-second real-time play recommendations
The Inversion
Traditional pipeline: football knowledge → tagging → summary → decision
THIRD & 20 pipeline: motion → state → constraint → decision
Football knowledge lives in interpretation/UI layer, NOT the sensing layer. This is why public YouTube film becomes valuable - you're not asking "what plays did they run" but "what constraints does this team operate under."

Conclusion
The CV pipeline has successfully built the core differentiating metrics (SDI, Recognition Latency, First Step Quickness) that no competitor offers. However, several catalog claims are currently unsupported, and the broader vision of a neutral fact-finding platform requires substantial additional development.
The path forward is clear: first fix what's been promised, then build the physics-first neutral backbone, then create the preseason scouting engine, and finally deliver the 12-second play recommendation capability that will truly differentiate THIRD & 20 in the market.
The high school market is the right entry point - these schools cannot afford analytics staff or manual tagging, but they can afford a fixed hardware + software system that replaces labor with computation. The neutral, physics-first approach is not just "cleaner" - it's the only way the real-time promise is even feasible.
